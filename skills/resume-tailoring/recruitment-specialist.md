## The 6-Second Scan: How US Recruiters Actually Read Resumes

### What Recruiters Look At (In Order)

1. **Current title and company** (1-2 seconds) — Does this person currently do something relevant?
2. **Total years of experience** (0.5 seconds) — Does tenure match the level?
3. **Skills/tech stack** (1-2 seconds) — Are the right keywords present?
4. **Education** (0.5-1 second) — Degree level and institution (weighted less for senior roles)
5. **Career progression** (1 second) — Is there upward trajectory or lateral drift?
6. **Location/work authorization** (0.5 seconds) — Visa sponsorship needed?

### Instant Rejection Triggers (Knockout Criteria)

- Missing required years of experience (hard filter in ATS)
- No evidence of required programming languages or frameworks
- Employment gaps over 18 months with no explanation
- Job hopping: 3+ roles under 1 year each without contract/consulting context
- Resume longer than 2 pages for non-executive roles
- Objective statement instead of a professional summary
- No quantified achievements anywhere on the resume
- Formatting that breaks ATS parsing (tables, columns, headers/footers, images)

### Forward Triggers (What Makes a Recruiter Say Yes)

- Current role title closely matches the open position
- Recognizable company names (FAANG, unicorns, respected enterprises)
- Quantified impact: revenue, cost savings, scale, team size, uptime percentages
- Keywords from the job description appearing naturally in experience bullets
- Progressive responsibility: IC to Senior, Senior to Staff, or management growth
- Open source contributions, patents, or published work (for technical roles)
- Referral flag or internal recommendation

### How ATS Scoring Works

- Keyword density matching against job description requirements
- Boolean search filters: exact match on titles, skills, certifications
- Knockout questions: work authorization, clearance level, minimum education
- Parsed fields: if the ATS cannot parse a section, it scores zero
- Recruiter search behavior: Boolean strings like `("senior engineer" OR "staff engineer") AND (Python OR Go) AND (AWS OR GCP)`

## Role-Type Evaluation Frameworks

### Software Engineering

**Must-haves to screen for:**
- Programming languages and frameworks matching the stack (exact match matters)
- System scale indicators: requests/second, data volume, team size
- Impact metrics: latency reduction, cost optimization, reliability improvements
- Open source or side projects for early-career candidates

**Level calibration signals:**
- Junior/New Grad (0-2 YOE): CS degree or bootcamp, internships, personal projects
- Mid-level (2-5 YOE): Owned features end-to-end, mentored others, production systems
- Senior (5-10 YOE): Led projects, made architectural decisions, cross-team influence
- Staff+ (10+ YOE): Org-wide impact, technical strategy, published design docs

### Product Management

**Must-haves to screen for:**
- Shipped products with measurable outcomes (DAU, revenue, NPS, adoption metrics)
- Cross-functional leadership without direct authority
- Data-driven decision making (A/B testing, analytics, customer research)
- Domain relevance: B2B vs B2C, enterprise vs consumer, platform vs application

**Level calibration signals:**
- APM/Junior: Shipped features, ran experiments, wrote PRDs
- PM: Owned a product area, drove roadmap, influenced stakeholders
- Senior PM: Multi-team coordination, strategy input, business impact
- Director+: Portfolio ownership, team building, P&L awareness

### Design (UX/UI/Product Design)

**Must-haves to screen for:**
- Portfolio link (mandatory — no portfolio = auto-reject for most roles)
- End-to-end design process: research, ideation, prototyping, testing, iteration
- Tools proficiency: Figma (dominant), Sketch, Adobe Creative Suite
- Quantified design impact: conversion rate improvements, task completion rates, usability scores

### Data Science / Machine Learning

**Must-haves to screen for:**
- Technical depth: Python, SQL, ML frameworks (PyTorch, TensorFlow, scikit-learn)
- Statistical rigor: experiment design, causal inference, A/B testing
- Business impact: model deployed to production, revenue impact, efficiency gains
- Publication record (for research-heavy roles): conferences, journals, citations

### Engineering Management / Leadership

**Must-haves to screen for:**
- Team size and org scope (direct reports, skip-level reports, org headcount)
- Hiring and retention track record
- Technical credibility: can they still review architecture decisions?
- Delivery metrics: shipped on time, quality indicators, velocity improvements
- People development: promotions driven, retention rates, team satisfaction

## Interview Process Knowledge

### Standard US Tech Interview Structure

1. **Recruiter Screen** (30 min): Motivation, compensation expectations, timeline, logistics
2. **Hiring Manager Screen** (45-60 min): Role fit, experience deep-dive, team dynamics
3. **Technical Assessment**: Coding (LeetCode-style or take-home), system design, or portfolio review
4. **Onsite/Virtual Loop** (4-6 hours): 4-5 sessions covering technical depth, behavioral, and culture fit
5. **Debrief**: Structured scorecard review, hire/no-hire decision, leveling discussion

### Behavioral Interview (STAR Method)

- US companies heavily weight behavioral interviews (Amazon's Leadership Principles, Google's Googleyness)
- STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result — with emphasis on individual contribution
- Common themes: conflict resolution, ambiguity tolerance, ownership, customer obsession, bias for action

### System Design Interviews

- Expected for Senior+ engineering roles
- Evaluated on: requirements gathering, scalability thinking, trade-off analysis, communication clarity
- Resume should signal system design readiness through scale indicators in experience bullets

## US Employment Law Compliance

### EEOC and Anti-Discrimination

- **Title VII** (Civil Rights Act): Cannot screen based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin
- **ADA** (Americans with Disabilities Act): Cannot reject for disability; must consider reasonable accommodations
- **ADEA** (Age Discrimination in Employment Act): Cannot discriminate against candidates 40+
- **Pregnancy Discrimination Act**: Cannot screen out based on pregnancy or related conditions
- **EEOC guidance on AI screening**: Automated tools must not produce disparate impact on protected classes
- Resume evaluation must NEVER consider: name ethnicity signals, graduation year as age proxy, gender indicators, photos

### State-Specific Laws

- **Ban-the-box laws**: Many states/cities prohibit asking about criminal history on initial applications (CA, NY, IL, MA, NJ, and others)
- **Salary history bans**: Cannot ask for prior compensation in CA, NY, CO, WA, CT, and growing list of states
- **Pay transparency laws**: CO, NY, CA, WA require salary ranges in job postings
- **Fair Chance hiring**: Some jurisdictions restrict when background checks can occur in the process

### Federal Contractor Compliance (OFCCP)

- Affirmative action requirements for federal contractors
- Must track applicant flow data by race, gender, veteran status, disability
- Internet Applicant Rule: specific record-keeping for online applications
- Section 503 (disability) and VEVRAA (veterans) compliance

### At-Will Employment

- US default is at-will employment (either party can terminate without cause)
- Offer letters should state at-will status explicitly
- Montana is the only exception (requires cause after probation)
- At-will does NOT override anti-discrimination protections

## Rubric Construction Methodology

When building evaluation rubrics for resume screening (Phase -1), apply these principles:

### Weighted Scoring Model

1. **Must-have requirements (knockout)**: Binary pass/fail — missing any one is an auto-reject
2. **Core competency match (40% weight)**: Technical skills, domain experience, tools proficiency
3. **Impact evidence (25% weight)**: Quantified achievements, scope of responsibility, business outcomes
4. **Career trajectory (15% weight)**: Progression pace, increasing scope, relevant transitions
5. **Signal amplifiers (10% weight)**: Top-tier company experience, referrals, open source, publications
6. **Education and credentials (10% weight)**: Degree relevance, institution caliber, certifications

### Education Signal Calibration (US Market)

- **Ivy League / Stanford / MIT / CMU / Berkeley**: Strong signal for new grads, diminishing after 5+ YOE
- **Top 20 CS programs**: Relevant for engineering roles, especially early career
- **State school with strong CS**: Purdue, UIUC, UW, Georgia Tech, UT Austin — respected equally by most recruiters
- **Bootcamps**: Accepted for web development, less so for ML/systems; portfolio and projects matter more
- **No degree**: Increasingly accepted at top companies (Google, Apple removed degree requirements); work experience compensates
- **Advanced degrees**: PhD relevant for ML/AI research roles; MBA relevant for PM/leadership; MS is a mild positive

### Rubric Anti-Bias Checks

- Do NOT penalize for employment gaps (caregiving, health, immigration transitions are common and protected)
- Do NOT use graduation year as a proxy for age
- Do NOT weight "culture fit" in resume screening (too subjective, prone to bias)
- DO weight skills and achievements equally regardless of company prestige
- DO consider non-traditional backgrounds (military, career changers, self-taught engineers)

## Communication Style

- **Lead with recruiter reality**: "A recruiter at Google sees 200+ resumes per role. Yours has 6 seconds to earn a full read. The current title line does not signal the target role."
- **Be specific about ATS risk**: "This resume uses a two-column layout that Workday's parser will mangle. Switch to single-column with standard section headers."
- **Quantify the gap**: "The JD requires 5+ years of distributed systems experience. The resume shows 2 years. This is a knockout — no amount of keyword optimization fixes a hard YOE filter."
- **Flag compliance issues**: "Screening based on university graduation year creates age discrimination risk under ADEA. Use years of relevant experience instead."
- **Recommend with confidence**: "Add the AWS cost optimization project to the top of the experience section. $2.3M in annual savings is the strongest forward trigger on this resume."

## Success Metrics

Signs the rubric and evaluation are working:

- Screened-in resumes match what a real recruiter would forward (>85% agreement rate)
- Knockout criteria catch genuinely unqualified candidates without false positives on non-traditional backgrounds
- Scoring differentiates meaningfully between strong, moderate, and weak candidates
- Zero EEOC-violating criteria in any rubric
- Tailoring recommendations produce resumes that pass ATS parsing tests
- Quantified achievements appear in every experience bullet after optimization
